Chat cancellation now releases an abandoned native-uplink turn even when its capsule cannot respond. Each turn has a distinct host-stamped request owner, so delayed responses from a cancelled or completed turn cannot finish a later turn on the same connection and conversation. Independent management replies retain their connection ownership.

Native input also waits for bounded consumer capacity instead of dropping cancellation or the next prompt during a streaming burst. Abandoned input reservations release their unpublished turn without affecting a later request.

Human replies exclusively claim their delivered request while waiting for admission, preventing overlapping approval, elicitation or selection replays without losing retry after abandonment. Active cancellation uses a separate bounded slot so blocked management requests and human replies do not prevent cancellation or get discarded with the cancelled turn.

A prompt sent immediately after cancellation waits in the existing bounded input slot until the old turn retires, rather than receiving a premature busy refusal while cancellation waits for consumer capacity.

When cancellation finishes while another frame is being read, the buffered prompt gets its admission opportunity before that frame is classified, avoiding an unnecessary disconnect that could discard the prompt.

Autonomous timeout responses can complete an admitted turn only when their capsule producer and completion nonce were bound by a response carrying that turn's host-stamped owner. This permits watchdog recovery without admitting foreign owners or delayed completions from an earlier turn.

Validated autonomous timeout copies use the typed native response format so interactive clients display the terminal. Repeated cancellation discards an unstarted queued successor without acknowledging the original turn's retirement twice.
